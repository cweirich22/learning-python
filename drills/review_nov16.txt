QUESTIONS
1. A child script runs `sys.exit(3)`. Without `check=True`, what happens, and where do you see the 3? 
   With `check=True`, what happens instead?
2. Why make an `os.environ.copy()` and change the copy, instead of passing `env={"REORDER_POINT": "15"}` 
   or editing `os.environ` directly? Give two reasons.

ANSWERS
1. Without `check=True`: the parent doesn't react at all. The child exits 3, `subprocess.run` hands back 
   the receipt, and your code keeps going. The 3 just sits in `result.returncode`, and you have to check 
   it yourself.
   With `check=True`: any nonzero code, 3 included, makes Python raise `CalledProcessError` in the parent. 
   If you don't catch it with `try/except`, the parent crashes, and `e.returncode` holds the 3.
   To remember it: `check=True` means "treat anything other than 0 as an error." It's the opposite of 
   passing through.
2. - Changing the copy doesn't touch the parent's real environment. (Yours.)
   - `env={"REORDER_POINT": "15"}` gives the child only that one variable. It loses everything else 
     it needs, like PATH and SYSTEMROOT on Windows, so Python or other programs may fail to start. The 
     copy keeps all of them and adds yours.
   - Editing `os.environ` directly would also change it for every later child the parent starts, which 
    leaks the setting into places you didn't mean.
